# Jonah — music and sound effects

Generated by `tools/story/music_jonah.py` from `assets/jonah/timeline.json` re-timed to the zh clips (see "Re-timed play and voice clarity"; finishing helpers in `tools/story/music_polish.py`, the voice-over-bed measurement in `tools/story/voice_margin.py`). Do not hand-edit the audio files;
change the script and rerun it:

```
python3 tools/story/music_jonah.py                          # music + sfx + preview + the tables below
python3 tools/story/music_jonah.py --skip-music --skip-sfx  # preview only (e.g. after new voice clips)
python3 tools/story/music_jonah.py --only storm,deep        # re-render some cues, keep the others' manifest rows
python3 tools/story/music_jonah.py --stems                  # also print per-stem levels (for balancing)
python3 tools/story/music_jonah.py --spec /tmp/jonah_spec   # also write a spectrogram PNG of every output
```

Same toolchain as the other packs (`mido numpy scipy`, `fluid-synth`, `ffmpeg`, macOS's `gs_instruments.dls`). The
script imports the shared engine `tools/story/music_kit.py` and does not change it. A full run takes about 2 minutes.
Scratch goes to `/tmp/jonah_music/` (`--tmp`), emptied after every cue and removed at exit. Seeds are per cue / per
effect (`jonah/<id>`), so reruns are reproducible and changing one cue never changes another. The tables between the
`<!-- …:start/end -->` markers are rewritten by the script.

**The timeline is timed by the real voices (`timedFrom` below). If it is re-timed again, rerun this script** so the
in-cue hits follow the new times: every hit is looked up by event (see "Timeline hooks"), and the script asserts that
every `music.cue` and `sfx.id` has a composer / synth and that every cue file is at least span × 1.3 + 0.6 s.

## Listen first

Nobody listened before this was built; the checks are measurements and spectrograms. Please listen in this order:

1. **`mercy`, from 9 s in (heart glow, 201.6 s zh)**: the call theme (horns) and the gentle Jonah theme (bassoon +
   clarinet, F major) at the same time. If they clash, the harmony of Jonah's bars 2-3 is the place to fix.
2. **`call`, first 25 s**: the call figure on the cello under the narrator, the full theme at the golden light
   (6.4 s, held 5 dB under God's first words), and Jonah's "No way!" at 15.6 s cutting it off with the grumpy bassoon. Then
   S08 (124.6 s): the same file is cut after 9.3 s; the theme enters at 6.4 s, under Jonah's "yes" (`jn_20`). Check the
   cut into `nineveh` is not abrupt.
3. **`storm`**: pacing and loudness. It must feel exciting, not scary; check the quiet S04 stretch (S04, bassoon
   confessing) and the big chord exactly on the splash (71.6 s zh). The thunder SFX hits should sit on timpani hits.
4. **`plant`, from 181 s**: the tuba chomps with the `munch` SFX, then the trombone "wah-wah-wah-waaah" droop. These
   comic gestures are the likeliest to read wrong without a listen.
5. **`shore`, 121.0 s**: the trombone slide + xylophone tumble on the "pfft" next to the `spit_pop` SFX.
6. **SFX `gulp`, `burp`, `munch`, `spit_pop`** (creature sounds are the weakest part of synthesis, pitfall M4): short
   and comic by design; judge whether they are funny or just odd.
7. **`deep`**: the cello "whale calls" (pitch-bent) and whether the hush stays under the narration.
8. `preview_mix.mp3` start to end for balance: it is the zh play mixed as the engine mixes it (music 0.8 x duck 0.5, SFX undocked).
9. The new level work: is the score still warm and present in the gaps, and do the swells on the hits still feel big while
   the underscore sits behind the voice? (`DIPS` and the 7 dB underscore are the knobs.)

## Formats (CONTRACT section 7)

- `assets/jonah/audio/music/<cue>.wav` and `audio/sfx/<id>.wav`: 44.1 kHz, mono, 16-bit PCM; a 96 kbps mono `.mp3`
  next to each (MP3 padding adds ~0.03 s; use the WAV lengths for timing).
- Music: −20.9 LUFS integrated over the composed span (EBU R128 via ffmpeg `ebur128`; the contract's −20 ±1, at its quiet end since 2026-11-15), loop bed −25.4, true peak
  ≤ −1 dBTP. Everything below 45 Hz is removed (phones cannot play it and it eats loudness).
- SFX: sample peak −3.0 dBFS. `gainDb` in `mix.json` is the recommended playback gain on top of that.

## Surviving a stretched section (re-timing at load)

As Ruth and Daniel: every cue's composed music ends on its cadence at `loopFromSec` (= the span it was composed for) and
continues into a quiet bed on its final chord, ~3 dB under the cue. `[loopStart, loopEnd)` (seconds, plus exact
`loopStartSample` / `loopEndSample`) is one repeat of that bed, whole bars and at least 4 s; the repeats are
**sample-identical tiles**, so a jump from loopEnd to loopStart is the same signal as playing straight on. Without
looping, just keep playing: every file runs at least 30 % past its span and fades over its last 2.5 s.

**`call` plays twice** (S01 at 0.0 and S08 at 124.56, zh play). One file, composed for the longer S01 (`composedFor` /
`composedSpan` in `cues.json`); at S08 it plays from its start. Its first 6.4 s are the figure on the cello and a
quiet pad; the full theme arrives at 6.4 s (the golden light) and is cut by the crossfade into `nineveh` at 133.83. `cues.json` has one
row per occurrence.

Ambience: `rain_loop` is a **whole-file seamless loop** (`loop: true`, loopStart 0, loopEnd = length, wrap crossfaded
with an equal-power 0.6 s fold), `stopAtCue: deep`, `fadeInSec` 1.0, `fadeOutSec` 1.5; `rain_stop` is the one-shot
that ends it (timeline 95.1 s). `cicadas_heat` is **not** a loop: a 9.5 s one-shot that fades in over 1.5 s and out
over 2.5 s, enough for the 6.7 s before `mercy`.

## The two themes

Both are original (`CALL` and `JONAH` in the script) and written so that they fit together.

**The call theme** (God's voice): a calm rising four-note figure **C-F-G-Bb** in F major, then the tune eases down
(Bb-A, G-A-F-G, F). Always on warm instruments: horn and cello, with choir, strings and harp.

**The Jonah theme**: a heavy, plodding tune in **D minor**: repeated notes, a stubborn step up, a sigh down
(D D D F D / E E E G E / F G A Bb / A G F D). Bassoon, with the tuba under it. `jonah(major=True)` gives the same
degrees in **F major** (D→F, E→G, F→A, G→Bb, A→C, Bb→D), which fits the call theme's chords.

| `runaway` (S02) | the Jonah theme **sneaks**: staccato bassoon under the pizzicato tiptoe |
| `storm` (S04) | the whole Jonah theme, slow and low, on the bassoon as he confesses, with a solo cello |
| `prayer` (S06) | the call theme from far away (muted horn, choir), the solo cello answers; at the prayer glow the cello sings all of it |

## How the score is made

- Composed as MIDI, one stem per instrument, rendered dry by FluidSynth, mixed in numpy with per-stem gain / filters /
  reverb send into one shared synthetic hall; mono; tempo stretched a few percent so whole bars fill each span;
  humanised onsets and velocities, delayed vibrato, expression swells; winds, bowed strings, voices and brass get
  Ruth's NRPN sustain fix so long notes hold.
- **Loudness footing:** the macOS bank plays choir, ensemble strings, xylophone, celesta and glock 10-25 dB quieter than
  cello / horn / bassoon at the same velocity (measured per stem). `TRIM` in the script lifts each program, so the
  "real choir-like pads" are audible, not buried.
- **Pad never drops out:** every pad chord is held until the next one starts (a gap shows up as a vertical dropout in
  the spectrogram; the scan in "Checks" covers it).
- **Comedy is written in:** pitch-bend parts (`BPart`, ±12 semitones) give the trombone slide on the "pfft", the
  clarinet "pffft" at the steam, the sinking bassoon at the gulp, the trombone "wah-wah-wah-waaah" droop in `plant`, the
  cello whale calls in `deep`. Hard hits are placed by timeline events: the big chord on the splash, timpani + crash on
  each thunder, the tuba chomps on the `munch`.
- **Storm without horror:** D minor with consonant chords only (Dm, Bb, Gm, A), a galloping cello / bass ostinato,
  tremolo violins, horn punches; no clusters, no screeching strings; the loudest moment is a short D minor chord.
- **Middle-Eastern colour without pastiche** (`nineveh`): the D hijaz scale (D Eb F# G A Bb C) on a shanai-like reed,
  koto standing in for the qanun, congas / bongos for frame drums, a drone on D.

## Re-timed play and voice clarity (2026-11-15 pass)

**What the cues are timed to.** `assets/jonah/timeline.json` is timed to the *longer* of the zh / en clip per line (233.2 s), but
the phone retimes at load to the language's own clips (`StoryRetime`, anchors). The Chinese play is 213.3 s, the English
219.0 s. The script therefore builds the **zh re-timed play** (`voice_margin.lang_timeline`, = `build_timeline.build`
with the zh clip lengths) and composes every cue span and every hit for it; `cues.json` `t` / `span` are zh times. Hits are
looked up by event as before. The English play uses the same cue files: where its events fall at a different place in the
cue, the hit is early or late by the drift below (a cue is never composed twice; the loop bed covers a longer section, a
shorter section cuts the last phrase into the 0.6 s crossfade). Nothing was composed for the old 233 s / 270 s timeline.

**Why the voices were buried, and what changed.** From `StoryDirector.cs` (read 2026-11-15): a line plays at volume 1; music
= file x 0.8 x duck, duck 1 -> 0.5 while a clip plays (music is 8 dB under speech); **SFX one-shots and the SFX loop are
not ducked** (they play at `gainDb` on top of the voice; `sfxLoop` has no duck, and `fadeInSec` in `mix.json` is not read).
Cues are held at -20 +-1 LUFS by `check.py`, so the music cannot simply be turned down; the pass therefore:

- renders every cue at **-20.9 LUFS** (was -20.0; the contract floor is -21.0) and the loop bed at -4.5 dB under the span
  (was -3): `SPAN_LUFS`, `VAMP_REL_DB`;
- carves a **speech pocket** into every cue (-1.5 dB at 900 Hz, -3.5 dB at 2.4 kHz, wide): `music_polish.pocket`;
- shapes the score to **breathe**: `music_polish.underscore` holds it 7 dB under the story and lifts it only around its
  hits (the SFX / glows in `HIT_SFX` / `HIT_FX`), so after normalisation the underscore is quieter and the hits stay big;
- adds a few shallow **dips** under lines the score cannot get out of the way of (`DIPS`, picked from the lowest lines of the table below);
- fades every mid-story cue in over 0.3 s so it never cuts in on a note;
- shortens the SFX that sit on speech (`SFX_GAIN` 2-4 dB lower, `SFX_SHAPE` short tails: chime, thunder, splash, bubbles,
  gliss, door ...); the punchy front keeps its level.

**Measured** with `python3 tools/story/voice_margin.py jonah` (engine model, zh play; voice RMS over its active frames
against music + SFX in the same frames, 100 Hz - 6 kHz). It is a model, not the reviewer's cross-correlation residual
(which has a codec floor): on this scale Daniel's pack is median 12.2 dB, worst 8.2 (reviewer scale 8.3 / 5.3).
"Before" = the old cue files played at the re-timed times, as they were this morning.
