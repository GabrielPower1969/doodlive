# Ruth — music and sound effects

Generated by `tools/story/music_ruth.py` from `assets/ruth/timeline.json`. Do not hand-edit the audio files; change
the script and rerun it:

```
python3 tools/story/music_ruth.py                          # music + sfx + preview + the tables below
python3 tools/story/music_ruth.py --skip-music --skip-sfx  # preview only (e.g. after new voice clips)
python3 tools/story/music_ruth.py --only home,finale       # re-render some cues, keep the others' manifest rows
python3 tools/story/music_ruth.py --stems                  # also print per-stem levels (for balancing)
```

Same toolchain as Esther (`mido numpy scipy`, `fluid-synth`, `ffmpeg`, macOS's
`gs_instruments.dls`). A full run takes about 2.5 minutes. The script **imports `tools/story/music_esther.py` as a library** (Score / Part / mel /
groove, the FluidSynth renderer, the synthetic hall, loudness and file helpers, the sfx primitives) and does not
change it, so Esther's output is untouched. The tables between the `<!-- …:start/end -->` markers are rewritten by
the script.

**The timeline is still an estimate. When Voice lands and `build_timeline.py ruth` re-times it, rerun this script**
so the in-cue hits follow the new times. Cue lengths come from the `music` spans and every in-cue hit is looked up by
event (see "Timeline hooks"); it asserts that every `music.cue` and `sfx.id` has a composer/synth and that every cue
file is at least span × 1.3 + 0.6 s. Seeds are per cue / per effect (`ruth/<id>`), so reruns are byte-identical
(checked) and changing one cue never changes another.

## Surviving a stretched section (re-timing at load)

The app re-times the play at load to whatever voice clips are installed (family recordings can be longer), so a cue's
section can end up longer than the file was composed for. Every cue therefore has two safety nets, both in
`cues.json`:

1. **A seamless loop section.** The composed music ends on its cadence at `span` (`loopFromSec`). From there the cue
   continues into a quiet bed on its final chord (pad + contrabass + the cue's own figure: harp arpeggios, a soft oud
   walk for `journey`, a hand-drum groove for `wedding`, a pizzicato tiptoe for `sneaky`, a celesta bell for
   `finale`), ~3 dB under the cue. `[loopStart, loopEnd)` (seconds, plus exact `loopStartSample` / `loopEndSample`)
   is one repeat of that bed, 1–3 whole bars and at least 4 s. Loop it until the next cue starts, then crossfade as
   usual. The repeats are **sample-identical tiles** (the second repeat as rendered, which already carries the first
   one's ringing tails, its first 30 ms blended from the true continuation), so jumping from loopEnd to loopStart is
   exactly the same signal as playing straight on (checked: tile max difference 0, seam step = normal step). The
   cue's own ring and reverb are faded out before loopStart, so the loop never contains part of the cadence.
2. **A plain tail.** Without looping, just keep playing: every file runs at least 30 % past its span (+0.6 s; the
   actual figure per cue is in the table, 76–223 %) on the same bed, and fades out over its last 2.5 s.

This also applies to `finale`: after its held D major chord it continues on the bed and fades; when the story ends on
time the director simply fades it out.

Ambient beds (`birds`, `crickets_dusk`, `crickets_night`) are **whole-file seamless loops** (`loop: true`, loopStart
0, loopEnd = length, wrap crossfaded with an equal-power 0.6 s fold), independent of the timeline. `mix.json` gives
each one `stopAtCue` (the next music cue after it starts: birds → `boaz`, dusk → `night`, night → `wedding`),
`fadeInSec` 1.0 and `fadeOutSec` 1.5. **Integration:** the director's single `sfxLoop` source currently stops only
at the end of the story or when another loop starts, so without a stop at `stopAtCue` the night crickets would run
on through the wedding; the preview mix already plays them this way.

## Formats (CONTRACT section 7)

- `assets/ruth/audio/music/<cue>.wav` and `audio/sfx/<id>.wav`: 44.1 kHz, mono, 16-bit PCM; a 96 kbps mono `.mp3`
  next to each (MP3 padding adds ~0.03 s; use the WAV lengths for timing).
- Music: −20.0 LUFS integrated (EBU R128 via ffmpeg `ebur128`, iterative gain), true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.
- SFX: sample peak −3.0 dBFS. `gainDb` in `mix.json` is the recommended playback gain on top of that.
## How the reference episode informed it

The owner asked for the score, pacing and feel of the reference episode (local audio only, never uploaded; analysed
2026-11-14 with numpy: per-gap spectra, onset rate, beat autocorrelation, chroma key, top-line pitch track over the
11 speech-free passages of 25.6 min). Nothing was transcribed or reused from it: no melody, motif or sample. What it
gave us, by measurement:

| Measured in the reference | Used here |
|---|---|
| Music-only passages are sparse: 0.3–1.2 onsets/s, slow pulse ~50–70 bpm, the top line changes note every 1–2 s | Slow tempi for the emotional cues (road_sad 55, night 66, home 74, finale 83 bpm); melodies in long notes; percussion only in journey / field / boaz / wedding |
| Emotional passage (the Ruth-story bed) in D minor with an augmented-second colour; very dark mix (63 % of energy below 150 Hz, 1–5 % above 2 kHz) | Everything centred on D (D minor → D major Picardy on the hug; D Dorian, D major), a raised C# against Bb in the lament; warm low-passed mix: 0.6–7 % of energy above 2 kHz in every cue |
| Quiet reflective passages sit ~8 dB under the busy ones; the ending is the loudest, brightest and in D major | Dynamics follow the story: under-dialogue sections are thin, the theme blooms only at the hug, the blessing, the golden rays; finale is the fullest cue |
| Instrumentation by ear-proxy: low string bed, a solo melodic voice in the D4–A5 range, plucked lines, little high percussion | Low strings + contrabass bed, a solo voice per cue (reed, cello, clarinet, flute, music box), oud / qanun-like plucked lines, frame drum rather than kit |

## How the score is made

- As Esther: composed as MIDI, one stem per instrument, rendered dry by FluidSynth, mixed in numpy with per-stem
  gain / filters / reverb send into one shared synthetic hall; mono; tempo stretched a few percent so whole bars fill
  each span; humanised onsets, velocities, delayed vibrato, expression swells.
- **New for Ruth: sustained instruments actually sustain.** Measured 2026-11-14: as FluidSynth loads the macOS DLS
  bank, every instrument has a decaying volume envelope (a held flute note is ~60 dB down after 2 s, a cello after
  ~4 s, slow strings ~25 dB down after 4 s). Every wind, bowed-string and voice part here sends an NRPN generator
  offset (SoundFont gen 37 volEnvSustain −1440 cB) that restores a full sustain, so long reed, flute, cello and pad
  notes hold like players' notes and phrasing comes from length, velocity and CC11. (Esther's cues still have the
  decaying envelopes; porting the same 4-line `P()` helper into `music_esther.py` would be an Esther-owner decision.)
- Pads overlap the next chord slightly and only the first chord of a cue swells from low, so there is no pumping at
  bar lines (checked: no 0.5 s window below −36 dB inside any span except the intended "Who's that?" hush in `boaz`).
- The GM bank has no duduk, ney, oud, qanun or frame drum: English horn + a breath of clarinet (duduk-like reed),
  nylon guitar (oud), koto (qanun), congas (frame drum), as Esther.

## Known limits

- Checked by measurement (ffmpeg ebur128, spectra, 0.5 s level scans, a spectrogram of the preview), **not by ear**
  (written unattended). Listen first to: the English-horn "duduk" in `road_sad` (GM English horn is nasal; if it reads
  as oboe-ish, swap to clarinet-led), the stage-whisper balance of `sneaky` (pizz may be busy under Boaz's whisper),
  `cheer` (synthetic voices remain the weakest link), and the cricket beds (steady 4.4 kHz chirps; −18 dB gain may
  still be too present on a phone speaker).
- The full sustain makes long notes even and organ-steady; real players would taper. If a long note sounds static,
  shorten it or add a CC11 taper in the cue rather than removing the sustain fix.
- Lengths are estimates until the voices land: rerun after the re-time (the loop / tail only covers the stretch; the
  in-cue hits stay where the old timeline put them until the rerun). Replace any cue or effect with a recording later:
  same id, same format, same loop fields.
- Ambience loops repeat every 10 / 16 / 20 s; a long stretched night scene will reveal the owl repeating every 20 s.
