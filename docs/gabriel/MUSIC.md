# Gabriel — music and sound effects

Generated by `tools/story/music_gabriel.py` from `assets/gabriel/timeline.json`. Do not hand-edit the audio files;
change the script and rerun it:

```
python3 tools/story/music_gabriel.py                          # music + sfx + preview + checks + the tables below
python3 tools/story/music_gabriel.py --skip-music --skip-sfx  # preview only (e.g. after new voice clips)
python3 tools/story/music_gabriel.py --only fanfare,finale    # re-render some cues, keep the others' manifest rows
python3 tools/story/music_gabriel.py --stems                  # also print per-stem levels (for balancing)
python3 tools/story/music_gabriel.py --check                  # only measure the files on disk (table "Checks")
```

Toolchain as the other packs (`mido numpy scipy`, `fluid-synth`, `ffmpeg`, macOS's `gs_instruments.dls`); a full run
takes under a minute. The script **imports `music_esther.py` and `music_daniel.py` as libraries** and changes
neither. Scratch files go to `/tmp/gabriel_audio/music/` (`--tmp`), emptied after every cue and removed at exit.
Seeds are per cue / per effect (`gabriel/<id>`), so reruns are reproducible. The tables between the
`<!-- …:start/end -->` markers are rewritten by the script.

**After any re-timing (`build_timeline.py gabriel`), rerun this script**: every in-cue hit is looked up by event
(see "Timeline hooks"). The script asserts that every `music.cue` and `sfx.id` has a composer / synth and that every
cue file is at least span × 1.3 + 0.6 s.

## What makes this score different from the other packs

The brief (DESIGN.md "Music direction"; the owner, 2026-11-14): epic, warm, cinematic orchestra with choir, the
best-sounding score in the app. The other packs play the macOS DLS bank through FluidSynth only; its choir and
strings are thin and decay. Here three **synthesised sections are layered over the sampled instruments**
(`SynthPart` in the script; same notes API, CC 11 swells work):

| Section | How it is made | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Choir** | 6 singers per note (own detune ±9 cents, slow pitch wander, delayed 5 Hz vibrato), a band-limited glottal wave; men (below B3) and women through their own formant banks ("ah" 700 / 1150 / 2600 / 3300 Hz, "oh" for the far choir; women's formants 17 % higher), breath noise that follows the singing | every cue; the effect `choir_ah` |
| **String ensemble** | 6 desks per note (detune ±6 cents, own bowing attack, delayed vibrato), body resonances (290 / 480 / 1150 / 2700 Hz), a trace of bow noise | every cue; carries the tune in `finale` with the DLS violins |
| **Brass section** | FM brass (1 : 1, brightness follows the breath, a small pitch scoop on the attack, 3 players) under the DLS horns and trumpets | `heaven`, `story`, `fanfare`, `finale`; inside `trumpet_call` |

Also: a soft suspended-cymbal swell (a cloud of 70 high partials, never a crash), a longer hall ("heaven", RT about
3 s, added to this process only), and a little air on top (+3 dB above 5 kHz). The sampled instruments still carry
the attacks: harp, timpani, flute, clarinet, celesta, glockenspiel, pizzicato, horns, trumpets.

## The Gabriel theme

One original melody (`THEME` in the script), D major, 4/4. Bar 1 is the call the design asks for: **A up a fourth
to D, up a fifth to A**, then one step higher to B (bar 2) and settling on F#. Bars 3-4 answer down to the dominant;
bars 5-6 call again and climb B-C#-D to the high tonic over IV - vi; bar 7 turns upward; bar 8 is home.

```
| A4 .  D5 A5 -  | B5 A5 F#5 -  | G5 . F#5 E5 D5 | E5 - - (rest) | A4 . D5 A5 -  | B5 . C#6 D6 -  | B5 G5 A5 C#6 | D6 - - - |
| D              | G     D/F#   | Em       A     | Asus    A     | D             | G        Bm    | G     A      | D        |
```

A rising fourth then fifth is the common language of fanfares (bugle calls live on those two intervals). What is
this pack's own is the rhythm (long, short, long), the step up to the sixth and the climb in bar 6. No bar is taken
from any film score; the reference in DESIGN.md lends only the mood.

| Where | How the theme appears |
|---|---|
| `heaven` | hinted: horns, an octave down, bars 1-2 in the last two bars, far away |
| `story` | the call alone in G major (D-G-D) on a horn when he points up to heaven |
| `gentle` | bars as they fit, softly on the flute over a cello line |
| `trumpet_call` (effect) | **stated**: A-D-A on the herald trumpet, then the climb B-C#-D, held |
| `fanfare` | the orchestra answers the trumpet's high D; the horns sing bars 1-2 back underneath |
| `finale` | full: violins in octaves, trumpets from the climb, horns and choir on the harmony |

When a section is too short for all 8 bars the script picks bars that still make a sentence (`SEQ`): e.g. 4 bars =
1, 2, 6, 8; 6 bars = 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8.

## Surviving a stretched section (re-timing at load)

As Daniel / Ruth: every cue's composed music ends on its cadence at `loopFromSec` and continues into a quiet bed on
its final chord, about 3 dB under the cue. `[loopStart, loopEnd)` is one repeat of that bed (whole bars, at least
4 s); the repeats in the file are **sample-identical tiles**, so a jump from loopEnd to loopStart is the same signal
as playing straight on. Without looping, just keep playing: every file runs at least 30 % past its span and fades
over its last 2.5 s. Beds: far choir "oh" + string ensemble + harp + celesta (`heaven`, `descend`, `gentle`,
`fanfare`, `finale`), pizzicato + harp (`story`).

**`gentle` plays twice** (S03 and S06). One file, composed for the longer section; at the other it plays from its
start and is crossfaded out. `cues.json` has one row per occurrence.

## Formats (CONTRACT section 7)

- `assets/gabriel/audio/music/<cue>.wav`, `audio/sfx/<id>.wav`: 44.1 kHz, mono, 16-bit PCM; a 96 kbps mono `.mp3`
  next to each (MP3 padding adds about 0.03 s; use the WAV lengths for timing).
- Music: −20.0 LUFS integrated over the composed span (EBU R128 via ffmpeg `ebur128`), loop bed −23, true peak
  ≤ −1 dBTP.
- SFX: sample peak −3.0 dBFS. `gainDb` in `mix.json` is the recommended playback gain on top of that.

that). Some energy stays between 300 and 900 Hz on purpose: a phone speaker plays almost nothing under 250 Hz.

## Checks (measured, not listened to)

Loudness and true peak by ffmpeg `ebur128`. **Loop tiles**: the largest sample difference between one loop repeat
and the next in the file (0 = identical). **Seam jump**: the step from the last sample of the loop to its first,
in units of the loop's own typical sample-to-sample movement (99.9th percentile); under 1.5 is inaudible.
**Click score**: the largest sample-to-sample jump in the whole file in the same units; a hard click scores far
above 10. **Theme**: the pitch of each note of `trumpet_call` against the score.

## Listen first

Nothing here was heard by a person: balance was set from per-stem levels and spectra. In this order:

1. `preview_mix.mp3` from S05 (the line "听——", the trumpet, the orchestra's answer): is the trumpet proud and
   clear, does the answer arrive exactly on its high note?
2. `sfx/trumpet_call.wav` alone: the DLS trumpet is the weakest sampled instrument in the bank; the FM layer is
   there to give it glow. If it sounds toy-like, this is the first thing to replace with a real recording.
3. `music/finale.wav` and `music/heaven.wav`: the synthesised choir and strings. They should read as a choir pad and
   a string section, not as an organ; if the choir is too "synth", lower `SYN_TRIM["choir"]` or raise the DLS choir.
4. `sfx/wing_flap.wav` on the phone speaker (it plays 5 times): big and soft, or too quiet on a small speaker?
   `gainDb` −5 is the loudest of the effects for that reason.
5. `music/gentle.wav` under the kneeling lines: the flute must stay under the voice.
