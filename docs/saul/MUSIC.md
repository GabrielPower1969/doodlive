# Saul Meets Jesus — music and sound effects

Generated by `tools/story/music_saul.py` from the timeline (see "Which timeline"). Do not hand-edit the audio files;
change the script and rerun it:

```
python3 tools/story/music_saul.py                           # music + sfx + preview + the tables below
python3 tools/story/music_saul.py --skip-music --skip-sfx   # preview only (e.g. after new voice clips)
python3 tools/story/music_saul.py --only light,finale       # re-render some cues, keep the others' rows
python3 tools/story/music_saul.py --stems                   # also print per-stem levels (for balancing)
python3 tools/story/music_saul.py --spec /tmp/saul_spec     # also write a spectrogram PNG of every output
```

Same toolchain and shape as `music_risen.py` (`mido numpy scipy`, `fluid-synth`, `ffmpeg`, macOS
`gs_instruments.dls`); imports `music_kit.py`, `music_polish.py` and `voice_margin.py` without changing them, and
imports `JESUS` / `JESUS_HARM` / `JOINT` from `music_miracles.py` (untouched), so Real Miracles, The King Who Serves,
He Is Risen! and this pack play one Jesus theme. A full run takes about one minute. Scratch: `/tmp/saul_music`
(removed at exit).

## Listen first

Nobody has listened yet; everything below was judged by measurement (1 s RMS profiles, loop checks, the
voice-over-bed engine model). Please listen in this order (times are the zh play in `preview_mix.mp3`):

1. **`light` (32.5 – 62 s)**, the heart of the story: the march breaks off, a high shimmering chord swells to the
   `light_whoosh` at 33.4 s, harp glissando, choir "ah"; then the slow flute Jesus theme under Jesus' words. The
   swell is the loudest second of the score (−15.5 dB RMS, softer than risen's stone, no drums, nothing low). Judge
   whether it is wonder and not a bang, and whether `light_whoosh` + `sit_thud` (34.5 s) read as light and a soft
   comic bump.
2. **`angry_march` (0 – 18.5 s)** and **`new_saul` (146 – 160 s)**: the same tune, minor and stomping, then major and
   skipping. Check that the minor march is comic-stern (not frightening for a 4-year-old) and that a listener hears
   they are the same tune. Same with `finale` (210.8 – 230 s): the tune in major, then the Jesus theme above it
   (JOINT harmony); check the two melodies do not fight.
3. **`ananias` (85 – 110 s)**: tiptoe + wobbling bassoon; the Jesus theme answering under both Jesus lines; the
   woodblock + bassoon "gulp" after "Saul?! THE Saul?!" (≈ 100 s). Must be comic and warm, not spooky.
4. **`brother` (110 – 137 s)**: hush for the three `knock`s (114 s), the Jesus theme in full from 115.5 s, the harp
   run on the scales (`sparkle_up`, 124.8 s) and the soft major-march hint for "You call me brother?".
5. **`basket` (160 – 190 s)**: the falling bassoon line across `rope_creak`, the soft `basket_land` pluck, the tuba
   answer after the `snore`. M4: the snore and rope creak are the weakest synthesis; they need ears.
6. **`barnabas`** (6/8): worried clarinet turning to a joyful flute at "Welcome!".
7. **`dark_walk`**, **`three_days`**: quiet; check they are not sad-scary, and that `three_days` hangs unresolved.
8. SFX: `march_steps`, `light_whoosh`, `knock`, `water_splash`, `snore`, `rope_creak`, `basket_land`.
9. `preview_mix.mp3` start to end for balance.

## Which timeline

Cues are **composed for the play as the phone runs it in Chinese** (`voice_margin.lang_timeline("saul", "zh")`,
230.0 s at the last run). It is the shortest play, so no cadence is cut off by the next cue. In-cue hits are looked
up by line id and event. Each file is long enough for its **longest** section across the zh play, the en play
(243.8 s) and `assets/saul/timeline.json` (252.5 s) × 1.3 + 0.6 s (`longestSpan` in `cues.json`); past its cadence
each cue continues into a quiet loop section on its final chord (`[loopStart, loopEnd)`, sample-exact, identical
tiles). **Rerun this script after any re-time or any change to event anchors in `story.py`** (the `basket` cue follows
`rope_creak` / `basket_land`, `brother` follows `knock` / `sparkle_up`, `light` follows `light_whoosh`).

## Formats (CONTRACT section 7)

- `assets/saul/audio/music/<cue>.wav` and `audio/sfx/<id>.wav`: 44.1 kHz, mono, 16-bit PCM, plus a 96 kbps mono
  `.mp3` next to each.
- Music: the composed span at −20 LUFS (+-1): `dark_walk` and `three_days` −21.0, `road`, `ananias`, `basket` −20.4,
  `new_saul` −19.7, `finale` −19.5, the rest −20.0. Loop beds 4.5 dB under their cue. True peak ≤ −1 dBTP. Below 45 Hz
  removed; the speech pocket (−1.5 dB at 900 Hz, −3.5 dB at 2.4 kHz); the score sits 7 dB down under the story and
  swells on `light_whoosh` (clear of speech in zh) and on the finale's last chord (`music_polish.underscore`).
  `DIPS` (by line id, resolved on every run): sa_02 −2.5 and sa_03 −3 (angry_march), sa_11 −2 (light), sa_19 −2
  (ananias), sa_24 −2.5 and sa_25 −2 (brother), sa_30 −2 (new_saul), sa_34 −2.5 (basket), sa_39 −3 (barnabas).
- SFX: sample peak −3.0 dBFS in the file; all **one-shots** sized to their scene (StoryDirector stops loops only at a
  music cue); `gainDb` in `mix.json` is the recommended playback gain. With those gains every effect plays at a
  peak of −8 dBFS or lower, the music's loudest peak is −3.5 dBFS, and each effect sits 4 – 10 dB under the music's
  RMS where it plays (`knock` is the exception: the music is hushed on purpose).
- `assets/saul/audio/preview_mix.mp3`: the zh play (42/42 lines) with music and SFX as `StoryDirector` mixes them.

## The themes

- **Saul's march** (`SAUL`, 4 bars, original, D minor, 4/4): D-D-F-A-G / F-F-G-A / Bb-Bb-A-G-F / E-E-D over Dm Dm Gm
  A-Dm. In **D major** it is the same tune with F → F# and Bb → B over D D G A-D. `angry_march`, `road` (slow, solo
  cello), `dark_walk` (in pieces, solo cello), `basket` (two bars of pizzicato / tuba, minor) are the minor face;
  `brother` (a soft hint on oud for "You call me brother?"), `new_saul` (skipping: off-beats a triplet late, flute +
  oud), `finale` (whole band) are the major face.
- **The Jesus theme**: `JESUS` / `JESUS_HARM` from `music_miracles.py`, unchanged. `light` (bars 1-4, slow flute),
  `ananias` (bars 1-2 and 3-4 as the answer), `three_days` (first four notes, A-B-D … E held, unresolved on Asus),
  `brother` (all 8 bars), `finale` (bars 1-4 over the march, on the `JOINT` harmony).
- `baptism` has its own happy 4-bar flute tune (D G A D); `barnabas` (6/8) has a worried B-minor clarinet figure
  that comes back major on flute.

## Weak or unverified

- **Not listened to by anyone.** All judgements are measurements.
- English play: the finale's last `sparkle_up` lands 2.5 s later than composed (20.4 s into the cue, after the final
  chord has started to fade); `knock` is 0.7 s later, `rope_creak` / `basket_land` 0.5 s earlier. Everything else
  is within 0.2 s.
- `light`: after the swell the score falls to about −34 dB RMS under the narrator (voice duck plus the 7 dB underscore),
  so the shimmer under "a light brighter than the sun" is faint; it is fully heard only in the first 3 s and in the
  Jesus-theme part (−26 dB). If it feels thin, raise the cue's `CUE_LUFS` or add a second hit.
- The two themes in `finale` (march in the flute / oud, Jesus theme on violins / choir) were checked on paper
  against the JOINT harmony, not by ear; a clash on the second beat of bar 1 (A against B) is possible.
- The synthesised `snore`, `rope_creak` and `water_splash` (M4) are the likeliest to sound artificial; `rope_creak`
  is the quietest effect (−34 dB RMS in the mix) and may be inaudible on a phone speaker.
- `angry_march` is the only drum-driven cue (bass drum + stiff snare); at 104 bpm under a 4-year-old's ears it could
  sound more military than comic.
