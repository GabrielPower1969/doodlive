# Exodus — music and sound effects

Generated by `tools/story/music_exodus.py` from `assets/exodus/timeline.json`. Do not hand-edit the audio files;
change the script and rerun it:

```
python3 tools/story/music_exodus.py                          # music + sfx + preview + the tables below
python3 tools/story/music_exodus.py --skip-music --skip-sfx  # preview only (e.g. after new voice clips)
python3 tools/story/music_exodus.py --only sea,song          # re-render some cues, keep the others' manifest rows
python3 tools/story/music_exodus.py --check --png /tmp/x.png # measure the written files (+ a spectrogram per cue)
```

Same toolchain as Ruth (`mido numpy scipy`, `fluid-synth`, `ffmpeg`, macOS's `gs_instruments.dls`); a full run
takes about 4 minutes. The script **imports `music_esther.py` and `music_ruth.py` as libraries** (Score / Part / mel /
groove, the renderer, the hall, loudness and file helpers, the sfx primitives, Ruth's split render for loop sections
and her cricket synth) and changes neither, so Esther's and Ruth's output is untouched. All music is original and
synthesised from MIDI through the GM bank; no samples from any film or recording. The tables between the
`<!-- …:start/end -->` markers are rewritten by the script.

**The timeline is still an estimate (0/36 voiced). When Voice lands and `build_timeline.py exodus` re-times it,
rerun this script** so the in-cue hits follow the new times. Every in-cue hit is looked up by event (see "Timeline
hooks"); the script asserts every `music.cue` and `sfx.id` has a composer/synth and every cue file is at least
span × 1.3 + 0.6 s. Seeds are per cue / per effect (`exodus/<id>`), so reruns are byte-identical and changing one cue
never changes another.

## Surviving a stretched section (re-timing at load)

Exactly as Ruth (`docs/ruth/MUSIC.md`): each cue's composed music ends on its cadence at `loopFromSec` and continues
into a quiet bed on its final chord, ~3 dB under the cue. `[loopStart, loopEnd)` (seconds, plus exact
`loopStartSample` / `loopEndSample`) is one repeat of that bed, whole bars, at least 4 s, built as sample-identical
tiles, so jumping from loopEnd to loopStart is the same signal as playing straight on (checked below). Without
looping, every file runs at least 30 % past its span and fades out over its last 2.5 s.

Loop beds per cue: `slavery` a soft work drum + ney on Dm; `desert` drone + bell tinkle on Am; `bush` tremolo shimmer
+ crystal on D; `palace` a soft oompah on D; `plagues` pizz tiptoe on Dm; `passover` / `sea` harp bed on D; `exodus`
oud walk; `chase` a timpani + horn pedal on D (the calm "stand still" chord); `song` the 6/8 dance (same tempo, same
bar grid); `finale` celesta bells.

One difference: **`song` keeps a fixed tempo** (6/8, ♩. = 112) instead of stretching to fill its span, so its
composed part is the whole number of bars that fits (`loopFromSec` = 11 bars = 11.786 s, the span is 12.42 s) and the
dance bed carries on the same grid from there.

`crickets_night` is a whole-file seamless 20 s loop (`loop: true`), `stopAtCue: exodus`, fade in 1.0 s / out 1.5 s
(the director needs the same stop-at-cue handling as Ruth's night crickets).

## Formats (CONTRACT section 7)

- `assets/exodus/audio/music/<cue>.wav` and `audio/sfx/<id>.wav`: 44.1 kHz, mono, 16-bit PCM; a 96 kbps mono `.mp3`
  next to each (MP3 padding adds ~0.03 s; use the WAV lengths for timing).
- Music: −20.0 LUFS integrated over the span (EBU R128 via ffmpeg `ebur128`), true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.
- SFX: sample peak −3.0 dBFS. `gainDb` in `mix.json` is the recommended playback gain on top of that.

## The freedom theme

One original 8-bar melody (`FREEDOM` in the script). Its head is a rising five-note figure **D–E–F♯–A–D**; in
**D Phrygian dominant** (E → E♭, B → B♭, C♯ → C) the E♭–F♯ augmented second gives it the Egyptian colour, in D major it
is plain and bright.

| Where | How |
|---|---|
| `slavery` (Miriam: "can you hear us crying?") | the figure first heard, **Phrygian dominant**, on a lone ney over a bare fifth |
| `bush` ("bring my people out") | horns hum the figure in Phrygian dominant |
| `bush` (Moses: "I'll go!") | the horn plays it in **D major** for the first time |
| `palace` (end of "Let my people go!") | horn answers with the Phrygian-dominant figure |
| `passover` | the ney plays theme bars 1–2 at half speed, Phrygian dominant, through the night |
| `exodus` (S08) | the walking tune, D major (bars 1, 2, 6, 7, 8) on flute + qanun |
| `sea` (S11, the walls stand) | **full statement**, D major (bars 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8), whole orchestra + choir |
| `sea` (relief after the splash) | the figure on the horn under the narrator |
| `song` (S13) | Miriam's chant opens with the same five notes in 6/8 |
| `finale` | warm full statement (bars 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 8), held D major chord |

Pharaoh has his own tune (`PHARAOH`): dotted, swaggering, D major with one cheeky flat sixth (B♭) on bassoon + tuba +
trombone. It struts in `palace`, grunts in `slavery`, and is twisted into D minor for `chase` and his charge in `sea`.

## Miriam's song: beat grid for the Voice role

`song` is in **6/8 at dotted quarter = 112** (contract §7 wrote "♩ = 112, 6/8"; the lead's brief for this role says
♩. ≈ 112, which is what a lively dance chant needs, so the grid below is ♩. = 112, i.e. eighth = 336 per minute). If
Voice wants the slower reading (♩ = 112 → ♩. ≈ 74.7), change `SONG_DQ_BPM` and rerun; nothing else depends on it.

The tune (bars 2–9, one note per syllable slot, D major), then the people's echo (bars 10–11):

Each two-bar phrase has five strong slots (eighths 1, 3, 4, 6 of the first bar + the downbeat of the second), which

(eighths 1 and 4), the darbuka "dum" on eighth 1. Line up the chant's stressed syllables with bar starts; the timeline
puts `ex_33` 1.6 s into the cue (≈ bar 2.5), so the first re-time should start her line at **bar 2** (1.071 s) and
the people's `ex_34` at **bar 10**.
